Hardware Architecture and Power Integration Strategy
Interference Between Qi, Magnets, and NFC: The physical layout of your magnetic dock, Qi wireless receiver, and NFC antenna introduces significant electromagnetic interference risks [1, 2]. You must validate that your docking magnets are kept out of the NFC antenna field and away from the center of the Qi coil to prevent detuning [1, 3]. Additionally, ensure the NFC reader is physically shielded from the noisy power circuitry of your primary display [4].
Power Budget and Deep Sleep Reliability: Your overall battery life will be heavily dominated by idle currents and wake frequencies [5]. Before committing to a PCB, validate the quiescent current of your chosen battery charger and power-path module [1, 5]. You must also prove that the event-driven NFC logic works—specifically, that the mechanical card presence switch can wake the MCU and perform a one-shot tag read without the PN532 reader needing to poll continuously [6, 7].
GPIO Pin Exhaustion and Wake Aggregation: Because you are combining 6 physical buttons, two displays, an NFC reader, and multiple wake sensors (PIR, bump, and card presence), exhausting your MCU pins is a major risk [8]. Validate that your I/O expander or ADC resistor ladder reliably handles the buttons [9]. Most importantly, test your wake aggregation circuit (such as diode-OR logic or an interrupt combiner) on a breadboard to ensure multiple signals can successfully share a limited RTC wake net without false triggers [1, 10].
Display Interface Complexity: The fast primary color display creates the biggest pressure on both your pin budget and your battery [7]. You should validate the high-bandwidth interface alongside your other peripherals and ensure that aggressive power-gating and backlight timeout controls work as intended [2, 6, 7].
ESPHome Support Maturity: You must verify that the specific hardware modules you select—particularly your primary display and any I/O expanders—have mature, stable driver support in ESPHome [2, 6]. While prototyping on the ESP32-S3 lowers this risk compared to the ESP32-P4, toolchain readiness must still be confirmed before manufacturing a custom board [6].
